# Procurement Policy

This policy sets out how the company buys goods and services. It applies to every employee who raises, approves or pays for a purchase, and to every purchase paid from a company cost center. All amounts are in South African Rand (ZAR).

## 1 Purpose and scope

### 1.1 Purpose

The purpose of this policy is to make sure that company money is spent with the right approval, at a fair price and with a clear record of who decided what. It protects employees as much as the company: a purchase made by the rules is never a personal risk for the person who made it.

### 1.2 Scope

The policy covers every purchase of goods, services and software, from a box of printer paper to a multi-year service contract. Petty cash purchases under R 500,00 follow the Petty Cash Procedure instead. Salaries, taxes and transfers between company accounts are not purchases and are out of scope.

## 2 Fiscal year and budgets

### 2.1 Fiscal year

The fiscal year runs from 1 March to the last day of February of the following calendar year. Fiscal year 2027 (FY2027), for example, starts on 1 March 2026 and ends on 28 February 2027. Budgets, spend reports and the approval limits of this policy all follow the fiscal year.

### 2.2 Budgets and cost centers

Every purchase is charged to one cost center. The budget of the cost center is reserved when a request is submitted and released again when the request is rejected or cancelled. A request that would take a cost center over its budget for the fiscal year cannot be approved until finance raises the budget or the request is reduced.

## 3 Purchase requests

### 3.1 Raising a request

Every purchase starts with a purchase request in the procurement system before anything is ordered. The requester names the cost center, the supplier, the items with their quantities and prices, and the business reason. An order placed before the request is approved is a breach of this policy and is reported to finance.

### 3.2 Splitting purchases

A purchase may not be split into several smaller requests to stay below an approval threshold. Requests for the same goods or services from the same supplier within 30 days are added together when the approval thresholds are applied.

## 4 Approvals

### 4.1 Approval chain

Every submitted request is approved by the line manager of the requester, within the manager's approval limit. When the total is above that limit, the next level up (the department head, then finance) approves in the manager's place. Larger requests need further approvals, as set out in 4.2. Nobody may approve their own request, even when their approval limit would allow it, and the system enforces this. An approver can approve, reject with a reason, or let the request escalate when they are away.

### 4.2 Approval thresholds

The approvals a request needs depend on its total amount including VAT. Every approver approves only within their approval limit; when the total is above it, the next level up approves instead:

| Total including VAT | Approvals needed |
| --- | --- |
| Up to R 20 000 | Line manager |
| Over R 20 000 up to R 200 000 | Line manager and department head |
| Over R 200 000 | Department head and finance, and the line manager when the total is within their approval limit |

The line manager approves every request within their approval limit; above it, the department head approves in their place. The department head approves every request over R 20 000, and finance approves every request over R 200 000. The thresholds are compared with the amount including VAT, never with the amount excluding VAT.

### 4.3 Escalation and reminders

An approver has two working days to decide. After that a reminder is sent, and when the approver still has not decided the step escalates to the next level. Escalation never skips the finance approval of a request over R 200 000.

## 5 Quotes and suppliers

### 5.1 Quotes

Software purchases (licences, subscriptions and software services) over R 100 000 including VAT need at least two written quotes from different suppliers, attached to the request, also when the software is bought from a preferred supplier. The requester explains why the chosen quote was preferred when it is not the cheapest. For other purchases a second quote is good practice but not required.

### 5.2 Preferred suppliers

The company keeps a list of preferred suppliers in the supplier catalog, with agreed prices and terms. Buy from a preferred supplier whenever one offers what is needed. Buying elsewhere when a preferred supplier offers the same item needs a short written reason in the request. A new supplier is added to the catalog by procurement after its company registration, tax number and bank details have been checked.

## 6 VAT

### 6.1 VAT rate and amounts

VAT in South Africa is 15 %. Every request shows the amount excluding VAT, the VAT and the total including VAT. The approval thresholds in 4.2 and the quote threshold in 5.1 are compared with the total including VAT. A supplier that is not registered for VAT charges no VAT; its total is then the amount itself.

### 6.2 Invoices

A supplier's invoice is paid only when it matches the purchase order and the goods received (three-way match). An invoice that does not match is held until the difference is resolved with the supplier.

## 7 Conduct

### 7.1 Gifts and hospitality

Employees may not accept gifts, hospitality or favours from suppliers or would-be suppliers worth more than R 500 per supplier per fiscal year. Anything of more value is declined, or declared to finance within five working days when it could not be declined. Cash or vouchers are never accepted, whatever their value.

### 7.2 Conflicts of interest

An employee who has a personal or financial interest in a supplier, or who is related to someone who works for one, declares it to their manager before taking part in any purchase from that supplier. They then take no part in choosing the supplier or in approving its requests or invoices. A conflict that was not declared is a disciplinary matter.

### 7.3 Breaches

A breach of this policy is reported to finance. Finance may cancel an order that was placed against the rules, and repeated breaches are handled through the disciplinary procedure.
